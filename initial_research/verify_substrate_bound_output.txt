
==========================================================================
1. SUBSTRATE EFFICIENCY -- knowledge bits per substrate bit
==========================================================================
Shannon bound: eta <= 1.0

scheme     bits/param  know/param     eta   headroom  source
fp16             16.0        2.00   0.125       8.0x  Allen-Zhu & Li, measured
int8              8.0        2.00   0.250       4.0x  Allen-Zhu & Li, no loss vs fp16
int4              4.0        0.70   0.175       5.7x  Allen-Zhu & Li, measured collapse
ternary           1.6        0.70   0.437       2.3x  EXTRAPOLATED from int4

Best MEASURED efficiency : int8 at eta = 0.250
Unexploited headroom     : 4x

The thesis without reference to language models: transformers store
knowledge at ~1/4 of the information-theoretic bound. Does a dynamical
encoding close that gap?

Note the inversion: ternary has the LOWEST bits/param yet the HIGHEST
eta. Denominating in parameters reverses the ranking of schemes.

==========================================================================
3. RETRIEVAL CAPACITY OF A DYNAMICAL SYSTEM
==========================================================================
For N = 10,000 units:

  Hopfield retrievable patterns : 1,380
    (Amit-Gutfreund-Sompolinsky, alpha_c = 0.138)
  Sparse-code address space     : 803 bits
  Raw substrate                 : 10,000 bits

  Address space and storage capacity are different quantities.
  A code can address 2^803 states and still store only 10,000 bits.

==========================================================================
4. WHERE THE THESIS CAN WIN -- structure, not volume
==========================================================================
On incompressible data no encoding beats another; Shannon forbids it.
That is why the flat dataset cannot test the thesis (mistake M5).

For 100,000 items:

  generator                        memoriser   structure-finder   ratio
  flat (incompressible)              5.10 Mbit                 --    1.0x
  K=64, dev=8 bits                   5.10 Mbit          1.40 Mbit    3.6x
  K=256, dev=12 bits                 5.10 Mbit          2.00 Mbit    2.5x
  K=1024, dev=16 bits                5.10 Mbit          2.60 Mbit    2.0x

  The ratio is ground truth from the generator, not a benchmark score.

==========================================================================
5. THE EXPERIMENT, STATED WITHOUT LANGUAGE MODELS
==========================================================================

  GIVEN   a substrate of S bits, and items from a generator whose true
          entropy H is known by construction

  MEASURE eta = (bits of generator content retrievable) / S

  COMPARE encoding-as-static-weights  vs  encoding-as-dynamical-state

  VARY    the latent structure of the generator, from incompressible
          to highly structured

  PREDICT eta_static  is flat in structure    (memorises either way)
          eta_dynamic RISES with structure    (exploits regularity)

  The claim under test is a SLOPE, not a scalar. Nothing here mentions
  tokens, attention, or next-token prediction: those are one possible
  read-out mechanism, not the object of study.
