
==========================================================================
1. PATTERN COUNT vs INFORMATION — the unit error in the root README
==========================================================================
C(10000,100)            = 10^241.8   (count of distinguishable patterns)
log2(C(10000,100))      = 803.3 bits  (payload ONE pattern can carry)

A pattern COUNT is an address space. Its LOG is the storage capacity.
10^241 patterns is not 10^241 bits of knowledge. It is 803 bits.

Upper bound regardless of encoding: an n-unit substrate holds <= n bits.
   n=   10,000 units, w=   100 active:  log2(C(n,w)) =        803 bits  | raw ceiling    10,000 bits
   n=  100,000 units, w= 1,000 active:  log2(C(n,w)) =      8,073 bits  | raw ceiling   100,000 bits
   n=1,000,000 units, w=10,000 active:  log2(C(n,w)) =     80,785 bits  | raw ceiling 1,000,000 bits

The SDR argument shows ADDRESSING is not the bottleneck. True, and never
was in dispute. It says nothing about how many BITS can be stored.

2. KNOWLEDGE CAPACITY OF A FIXED BRAIN (Allen-Zhu & Li: 2 bits/param)
==========================================================================
Weight budget in RAM: 6.5 GB = 52 Gbit
  ^ this is a HARD information ceiling: you cannot store more bits than you have.

precision   bytes/param      params    2b/param   actual (capped)
fp16               2.00       3.2B       6.5 Gb          6.5 Gb
int8               1.00       6.5B      13.0 Gb         13.0 Gb
int4               0.50      13.0B      26.0 Gb         26.0 Gb
ternary            0.20      32.5B      65.0 Gb         52.0 Gb  <- storage-bound

NOTE: Allen-Zhu verified 2 bits/param down to int8. Below int8 the law is
      NOT verified, and the storage bound binds first. Treat int4/ternary
      rows as UPPER bounds, not measurements. This is gate G-CAP.

==========================================================================
3. IS THAT ENOUGH? — what the ceiling actually buys
==========================================================================
Fixed-brain ceiling in 6.5 GB: 52 Gbit

  Allen-Zhu reference: 7B model           14.0 Gb   FITS
      they estimate this EXCEEDS English Wikipedia + textbooks combined
  English Wikipedia + textbooks           14.0 Gb   FITS
      per the above
  30B-token web corpus (long tail)       240.0 Gb   SHORT by 4.6x
      ~4 chars/token, ~2 bits/char compressed
  300B-token corpus (the Pile)          2400.0 Gb   SHORT by 46.2x

=> CONCLUSION THAT REVERSED THE ARCHITECTURE:
   A fixed brain resident in RAM holds Wikipedia-scale knowledge with
   3.7x headroom. The 200 GB external memory bank in the earlier
   design was over-engineered for anything short of web-scale long tail --
   and it moved knowledge onto the SLOWEST component in the machine.

==========================================================================
4. WHERE THE KNOWLEDGE SHOULD LIVE — bandwidth comparison
==========================================================================
tier                             bandwidth         holds   relative speed
fixed brain (RAM)                    84 GB/s         52 Gb             28x
external bank (SSD)                   3 GB/s      ~1600 Gb              1x

The earlier design put 97% of parameters on the tier that is 28x slower.
Dynamics-first puts the load-bearing knowledge on the 28x faster tier and
uses SSD only for what genuinely exceeds the information ceiling.

==========================================================================
Allen-Zhu baseline for a standard dense transformer : 2.0 bits/param
Question nobody has published an answer to:
  does a dynamic/Hebbian substrate (BDH/TTT-style) beat 2.0 bits/param?

  measured b/param                                       verdict
             0.5      KILL: dynamics is worse than plain weights

             2.0        parity - premise not proven, not refuted
